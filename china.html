The China Coffee Market

China's coffee market has moved from a novelty into one of the fastest-growing consumption categories anywhere in the world. A specialty and branded café culture has spread from Shanghai and Beijing into second- and third-tier cities at remarkable speed, led by domestic chains such as Luckin Coffee, Cotti Coffee and Manner Coffee, alongside international names like Starbucks, Costa Coffee and %Arabica.

What's changing fastest is the supply side. As Chinese consumers develop more sophisticated tastes, buyers are actively looking beyond traditional supply relationships for new origins, distinctive flavour profiles and direct trading channels — opening real space for international green bean suppliers, roasters, equipment makers and specialty brands.

The Market Opportunity

China's coffee market is growing at a compound annual rate of more than 20%, with consumption value projected to exceed ¥200 billion (RMB) by 2025 — driving strong demand for beans from new origins and with unique flavour profiles.

The biggest hurdle for outsiders is access. International supply chains into China remain complex, and many brands still lack a direct channel to reach domestic buyers. Brazil alone supplies 38% of China's coffee bean imports, with Vietnam a distant second at 19% — leaving real room for new origins and new suppliers to build a foothold.

The Event: HOTELEX Shanghai — World Coffee Flavor Map

HOTELEX Shanghai is Asia's largest hospitality and foodservice expo — 400,000 sqm and more than 4,000 exhibitors across 14 sectors, from coffee and tea to bakery, tableware and central kitchen equipment. It runs every spring at the National Exhibition and Convention Center in Shanghai, alongside the Shanghai International Tea & Beverage Expo, Expo Finefood Shanghai and the HOTELEX Coffee & Food Festival.

Inside the show, the World Coffee Flavor Map is the dedicated coffee trade platform — created specifically for green bean trade and cultural exchange, spanning green beans, roasting equipment, coffee machines and ancillary products and services.

A Show Within the Show

Although HOTELEX itself is a general hospitality and foodservice expo, the coffee side of it is a substantial show in its own right — not a side note to a bigger event. Coffee & tea span two full exhibition halls (1.2H and 2.2H), and cafés and coffee shops alone made up 12.5% of all trade visitors at the 2026 edition — the third-largest professional visitor segment on the entire show floor.

The organizers run it with its own identity: the World Coffee Flavor Map has its own dedicated brochure, its own buyer list of major domestic and international chains — Starbucks, Luckin Coffee, Costa Coffee, Manner Coffee, Cotti Coffee, %Arabica and Lavazza among them — and its own hosted-buyer matchmaking track built specifically for coffee and tea beverage chains. The HOTELEX Coffee & Food Festival is also listed as one of the show's officially concurrent exhibitions, alongside the Shanghai International Tea and Beverage Expo.

The coffee halls also host China's national qualifying rounds for several major competitions — the China Barista Championship, China Latte Art Championship, China Brewers Cup and China Coffee Roasting Championship (Shanghai Division) — run under competition formats established by World Coffee Events. These are China's national and regional heats: winners go on to represent the country at the global World Coffee Championships, which move to a different host city each year (Tokyo is confirmed to host the 2027 World Barista Championship).
